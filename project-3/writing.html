How to Write in the Horror Genre

First Off...What is Horror?

"Our present era is one in which the heart of culture is blowing hard upon a coal of fear, and the fascination is everywhere," writes Winetraub on the surplus of horror movies (Owen,2018). And what is horror? It is, self evidently, storytelling (regardless of medium) meant to instill fear or repulsion upon the reader/viewer/listener.


How to Write Horror

  1. Pick a subgenre
  2. Determine the Protagonist and Antagonist
  3. Start with your ending: who wins in the end, and why? Does the killer succeed, or is there a final girl?
  4. Ensure there is risk. No risk for your hero/victim = no sense of suspense or terror.
  5. Create an outline/map out the story.
  6. Write! Allow your first draft to completely suck. No one will read it but you!
  7. Self Edit. This can be by chapter or wordcount, whatever is managable to you. Don't be afraid to set it down before coming back to it. This will give you some new perspective.
  8. Get feedback and kill your darlings if you must. If a passage, character, or concept is boring or isn't relevant to the story, get rid of it.
  9. Repeat 6 through 8 until you're ready to publish. Don't know when you are? Get peer feedback.

Subgenres

Two overarching genres in horror is Supernatural and Psychological. These can overlap and are found in other subgenres. Subgenres tend to have their own tropes and themes.

  • Gothic
    • American Gothic- the vast unknown of American Wilderness, Taboos, Native Americans. See, Weyland, or The Transformation, by Charles Brockden Brown.
      • There is also the Southern Gothic subgenre.
    • Gothic- Gothic narratives placed in Europe tend to revolve around family/aristocracy, old ruins, taboos, monsters. See, Dracula, by Bram Stoker
  • Slasher- the dangers that lurk in suburbs or in rural towns, the danger of being an outsider OR a dangerous outsider lurking, the predicaments that bad teens get themselves into. See, John Carpenter's Halloween.
  • SciFi Horror- the horror of technology and the unknown. What's more terrifying, being alone in space, or something lurking in the great beyond? See, Alien.
  • Cosmic Horror- originating from Lovecraft, this genre that is connected
  • Post Apocalyptic- zombies, plagues, nuclear fallout. The fear of the collapse of civilization, the fear of bio-chemical warfare. See, The Stand, by Stephen King.

There are plenty more genres you can find here!

  • Activley Read. How does the passage affect you? How is the narrative consructed?

    • read a variety of literature, don't just subject yourself to horror. With horror, read a variety of authors and genres.
